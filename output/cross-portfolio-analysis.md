## 5. AI Infrastructure Stack Positioning

```
Layer 4: AI Applications (End Users)
  ├── Sierra AI (customer-facing AI agents — CX automation)
  ├── Glean (employee-facing AI search + agents)
  └── Okta (identity for AI agents — Auth0 for AI Agents, ISPM)

Layer 3: AI Development Platform
  └── Vercel (AI app deployment — v0, AI SDK, Next.js)

Layer 2: AI Inference Compute
  └── Cerebras (wafer-scale inference hardware + cloud API)

Layer 1: Foundation Models
  └── OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta (model providers)
```

**Supply chain dependencies:**
- Cerebras provides compute to OpenAI (Layer 2 → Layer 1)
- OpenAI provides models to Sierra, Glean, and Vercel (Layer 1 → Layers 3–4)
- Okta provides identity to all layers (cross-cutting)
- Vercel provides deployment infrastructure usable by Sierra/Glean (Layer 3 → Layer 4)

**Correlated risk:** All 5 companies depend on continued enterprise AI spending momentum. An "AI winter" or enterprise budget rationalization would hit all 5 simultaneously, though Okta (identity is non-discretionary) and Cerebras (hardware already sold) have partial insulation.

### 6.2 Standardized "No Proprietary Model" Framework

All three software AI companies (Sierra, Vercel, Glean) use third-party LLMs. The dossiers treat this inconsistently:

| Company | LLM Dependency | Dossier Framing | Actual Risk Level |
|---------|---------------|----------------|:-----------------:|
| Sierra | OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta (multi-model orchestration) | "LLM wrapper" — framed as critical vulnerability | HIGH — model quality directly determines resolution quality |
| Glean | Claude, Gemini, GPT (Model Hub) | "Model-agnostic" — framed as strength | MEDIUM — retrieval/ranking layer is proprietary; generation is commodity |
| Vercel | Anthropic, OpenAI (v0) | "AI application company" — framed as limitation | LOW — AI is a feature, not the core product; deployment platform exists without AI |

**Consistent framework:** The risk from LLM dependency scales with how central the model is to the value proposition:
- **HIGH** when the customer interacts directly with model output (Sierra — the AI IS the product)
- **MEDIUM** when the model augments a proprietary system (Glean — search + retrieval is proprietary, generation is commodity)
- **LOW** when AI is one feature among many (Vercel — v0 is AI-dependent but the deployment platform is not)

This framing is internally consistent and justifies the different treatments.

## 7. Competitive Interactions Between Portfolio Companies

### 7.1 Direct Overlaps
- **Glean ↔ Sierra:** Both compete for enterprise "AI agent" budget. A CIO allocating $5M/yr to AI agents might split between customer-facing (Sierra) and employee-facing (Glean), not fund both at full projected levels. TAMs should be discounted by 15–25% for budget cannibalization.
- **Okta ↔ Glean:** Glean lists Okta as a customer. Okta's identity platform provides the authentication layer that Glean's connectors rely on. Complementary, not competitive.
- **Vercel ↔ All:** Vercel's deployment platform is infrastructure that any of the other 4 could use. No competitive threat; potential customer relationship.

### 7.2 Shared Dependencies
- **OpenAI:** Customer of Vercel AND Cerebras; model provider to Sierra, Glean, and Vercel. OpenAI financial stress (currently $600B+ in commitments vs ~$13B revenue) is a correlated risk across the entire portfolio.
- **AWS:** Okta and Vercel run on AWS. Glean runs on GCP. Sierra and Cerebras have their own infrastructure. An AWS outage (Vercel experienced one in Oct 2025) affects 2 of 5 companies.
- **Enterprise AI budgets:** All 5 companies draw from overlapping CIO technology budgets. A macro spending freeze hits all 5, though Okta (identity is non-discretionary) has the most insulation.

### 7.3 Portfolio Synergy
An investor holding all 5 would own:
- The identity layer (Okta)
- The enterprise AI search layer (Glean)
- The AI deployment platform (Vercel)
- The AI customer experience layer (Sierra)
- The AI compute layer (Cerebras)

This represents a near-complete "AI infrastructure stack" — from silicon to application. The portfolio's correlated risk is enterprise AI spend; its diversification benefit is that each company's moat type is distinct.
